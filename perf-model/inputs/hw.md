# VR200 hardware constants for the K3.1 SOL model

Machine-readable: `hw.json` (generated by `hw_src/build_hw.py` from the raw logs in `hw_src/raw/`).
Device: NVIDIA VR200 (Rubin, CC 10.7 / sm_107), Vera Rubin NVL72, 4 GPUs per node.

The **measured** values come from Hecate batch-xdr on 2026-10-30, in one 16-GPU NVL72 block. The jobs were 913068 (1 node) and 913069, 913098 and 913203 (4 nodes).
- Scripts: `/lustre/fsw/coreai_comparch_inferencex/weizha/k31vr/hw/{hwbench,l2bench,ncclbench,symmbench,symm2}.py`
- Stack: torch 2.16.0.dev20261009+cu134, NCCL 2.32.3, triton 3.8, flashinfer 0.7.1.

The **spec** values come from NVIDIA's Vera Rubin NVL72 spec table, which NVIDIA marks "Preliminary… up to and subject to change". The table marks rows as dense where they are dense.

The **code** values come from measured constants in docstrings under `vllm/models/kimi_k3/nvidia`.

**How to use these numbers.** Compute the SOL with the spec column. Use the achievable column as the per-resource derate when you match microbenchmarks.

**The HBM ceiling depends on data placement.**
- Use 17.1 TB/s only for streams whose bytes are homed on the die of the SM that reads them. Expert weights already do this today; `place_experts` does it for the MegaMoE.
- Use 13.9 TB/s for everything else.

**FP8 and FP4 run at about 60% of spec.** These measurements used `_scaled_mm` and flashinfer kernels at a 2300 W power limit; the SM clock read 2280 MHz against a 2424 MHz maximum. The likely causes are clock or power limits, or immature sm_107 kernels. A ratio check supports this: spec FP8/BF16 is 4.4x but measured is 3.0x. Treat the 0.6 derate as a library-dependent number, not a hardware property.

**Spec rates per SM per clock (derived, consistency check only).**
- BF16: 8192 FLOP/clk/SM, which gives 4 PF at 2.30 GHz.
- FP8: 32768 FLOP/clk/SM, which gives 17.5 PF at 2.52 GHz.
- NVFP4: 65536 FLOP/clk/SM, which gives 35 PF at 2.52 GHz.

**Skinny (decode-shape) cuBLAS GEMMs stream weights below HBM SOL.** At K=7168, N=16384, M=16 to 128:
- BF16: 24.0-24.1 us, which is 9.76 TB/s of weight reads.
- FP8 rowwise: 16.1-16.3 us, which is 7.3 TB/s.

These are stock-library numbers. The SOL model should not use them.

## 3. Memory

- **HBM capacity.**
  - Spec: 288 GB.
  - nvidia-smi: Total 286524 MiB (279.81 GiB) and Reserved 1297 MiB. Free is 285228 MiB, which equals the 278.54 GiB torch reports.
  - vLLM startup: "Free memory on device (272.48/278.54 GiB)" in every engine log of `results/k31vr-frozen-sweep-20261114`. About 6.06 GiB goes to the context, NCCL and other overhead before profiling.
  - **Use 272.48 GiB per GPU in the capacity model.**
- **HBM bandwidth.**
  - Spec 22 TB/s (rack 1,580 TB/s / 72).
  - Attribute-derived: 16128-bit bus x 4.752 GHz x 2 = 19.2 TB/s. I could not confirm what these attributes mean.
  - Measured, full chip, default allocation:
    - Triton streaming read, 8 GiB: 13.93 TB/s.
    - torch copy (read + write): 13.88 TB/s.
    - fill (write): 8.40 TB/s.
    - `dies.py` says "12.6-13.6"; `die_local.py` says "~13.9".
  - Measured, die-local: ~17.1 TB/s per `die_local.py` (ledger row 10, VR200); `dies.py` says ~16.5.
- **Two dies.** Each die has its own HBM and L2. Default allocations interleave the dies every 4 KiB. The driver assigns 100 SMs to each die's green context.
  - A cold miss costs ~290 ns on the local die and ~455 ns on the remote die (`dies.py`).
  - Die-to-die bandwidth was not measured directly.
- **L2.**
  - Size: 132,120,576 B = 126 MiB, or 63 MiB per die if split evenly. The persisting maximum is 78.75 MiB.
  - Repeated-read bandwidth by footprint: 16-64 MiB gives 30-35 TB/s; 96 MiB gives 26; 128 MiB gives 18; 192 MiB or more is HBM-bound.
  - **Effective full-rate reuse footprint: about 64 MiB.**
  - L2-resident dependent round trip: 169 ns (`primitives.py`). With 16 loads in flight it is 380 ns.
  - The pointer-chase numbers from l2bench are internally inconsistent (TLB effects). Do not use them.
- **SM resources.**
  - 212 SMs.
  - Shared memory: 228 KiB per SM, 227 KiB per block.
  - 64K registers per SM.
  - Clock: 2424 MHz maximum, 2280 MHz observed.
- **Host.** Each superchip (2 GPUs) has 1.5 TB of LPDDR5X on the Vera CPU and 1.8 TB/s of NVLink-C2C (spec). This matters only for the KV offload tier.

## 4. NVLink and communication

**Topology.**
- `nvidia-smi topo` shows GPU0-3 at NV36 to each other: 36 NVLink6 links per GPU through the switch.
- There is no intra-node vs inter-node difference in any measurement:
  - Ping-pong to peers 1..15: 2.82-2.93 us.
  - NCCL p2p 1 MiB to peers 1, 4, 8, 12: 24.0-24.6 GB/s.
  - Remote bandwidth r0-r1 vs r0-r4: 1.094 vs 1.092 TB/s.
- **Model the 72-GPU rack as a flat switch.**

**Bandwidth.**
- Spec: 3.6 TB/s per GPU bidirectional, or 1.8 TB/s per direction.
- SM-driven symmetric-memory copies, 256 MiB:

  | Pattern | CTA/SM | Remote read | Remote write |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | pair r0-r1 | 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 | 282 / 556 / 1061 / 1063 GB/s | 1039 / 1071 / 1091 / 1094 |
  | pair r0-r4 (other node) | 8 | 1072 | 1092 |
  | ring, all 16 GPUs | 8 | 971 | 1070 |

  Reads need 4 or more CTAs per SM in flight to saturate.
- `ops/sp_all_gather.py` cites 1.25 TB/s achievable ingress. That push all-gather reaches 0.98 TB/s; the NCCL ring reaches 0.40.

**NCCL large-message bus bandwidth** (256 MiB, NVLS on; job c4). The busbw convention follows nccl-tests.

| n | AG | A2A | AR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 406 GB/s | 310 | 489 |
| 8 | 450 | 361 | 512 |
| 16 | 445 | 386 | 864 (NVLS) |

NCCL all-gather and all-to-all reach only 25-35% of the push rate. The SOL model should cost communication at the symmetric-memory push rate, and use NCCL only as the stock reference.

**Small-message latency** (16 KiB-1 MiB, eager).

| n | all-gather | all-to-all | all-reduce |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 (one node) | 21.5-24.4 us | 22.3-30.8 | 21.5-26.7 |
| 8 | 21.7-31.0 | 22.8-42.5 | 33.3-38.1 |
| 16 | 36.8-41.5 | 23.8-88.7 (64-89 at 512 KiB or more) | 47.6-66.7 |

- **CUDA-graph-captured NCCL is slower than eager.** n=4: AG 36.5 us, AR 41.5-45 us. n=16: AG 55-58 us, AR 66-82 us.
- The NVLS-off and LL protocol variants are within noise of the default (c5).
- The full per-size tables are in `hw.json` under `tables.nccl_c4_nvls` and `tables.nccl_c5_variants`.

**Symmetric-memory all-reduce** (torch symm_mem, eager, includes ~2.3 us launch; job c5).

| n | multimem (NVLS) | two-shot | one-shot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 14.7-22.5 us (16 KiB-1 MiB); 42 at 4 MiB | 15.3-21.3; 31.8 at 4 MiB | 15.7-38.6 |
| 8 | 14.8-19.9; 30.1 at 4 MiB | 21.2-28.4 | 21.7-73 |
| 16 | 15.1-19.8; 23.8 at 4 MiB | 30.6-37.3 | 50.8-262 |

The stock fused AR + RMSNorm in the drafter costs ~13 us per site at TP16 B1 (`draft/allreduce.py`).

**Latency primitives.**
- NVLink flag ping-pong, one way, sys-scope release/acquire: **2.86 us** (2.82-2.93). The first rep (134-247 us) is warmup.
- NCCL send/recv round trip at 4 KiB: 41-44 us. At 64 MiB, one way reaches 178 GB/s.

**Recommended communication cost for new methods (push over symmetric memory):**
`t = n_dependent_hops x 2.9 us + bytes_in_per_GPU / 1.25 TB/s` (derate to 1.09 for a single pair). SOL uses 1.8 TB/s, plus about 1 us of PDL/flag overhead per synchronising handoff.

## 5. Launch and overhead

- Eager tiny kernel: 2.27 us.
- CUDA graph: 0.78 us per node (1000-node graph, no PDL).
- PDL dependent release after the last CTA exits: 0.73-0.75 us. With unfenced NVLink multimem stores in flight it is 0.90-0.96 us (`oproj/prepared.py`).

## 6. Caveats and gaps

1. **FP32 SIMT rate is spec only.** It feeds the KDA recurrence term. If KDA SOL matters, an FFMA microbenchmark would be worth running.
2. **MXFP4 GEMM rate is assumed equal to NVFP4.** Torch could not run MXFP4 `_scaled_mm`.
3. **Die-local HBM 17.1 TB/s comes from a code docstring (ledger row 10).** Its raw log is not among the Hecate hw logs.
4. **The 1.25 TB/s ingress figure is likewise docstring-only.** The directly measured value is 1.09 TB/s (single pair, write).
5. **Many-to-one and all-to-all push bandwidth with all 16 GPUs active simultaneously was measured only as a ring** (1.07 TB/s write). Bisection under all-to-all over 72 GPUs is not measured; spec gives 260 TB/s / 72 = 3.6 TB/s per GPU.
6. **The spec numbers are preliminary** NVIDIA marketing figures. The 50 PF NVFP4 "inference" figure is not dense; use 35 PF.
